1. Nature of the WhatIf Experience

WhatIf is an AI-powered simulation platform. When you submit a question or scenario, WhatIf generates a structured, hypothetical exploration of possible outcomes using AI models and rule-based simulation engines.

Everything you see inside the WhatIf simulation experience is generated content. It is not real, it is not verified, and it is not a prediction of what will actually happen.

2. AI-Generated Content May Contain Errors

AI models, including those used by WhatIf, can and do produce outputs that are:

  • Factually incorrect
  • Outdated
  • Internally inconsistent
  • Incomplete
  • Plausible-sounding but wrong
  • Misleading
  • Biased in ways that reflect training data

The AI may misunderstand your prompt, misinterpret context, or generate outputs that appear confident but are not accurate. This is a known limitation of current AI technology.

3. Results Are Not Predictions

WhatIf does not predict the future. Simulation outputs, KPI projections, timelines, and narratives are illustrative models designed to help explore trade-offs and decision structures — not forecasts of real-world events.

Numbers shown in WhatIf simulations (such as percentage changes, economic impacts, or timeline projections) are generated outputs based on simplified rule-based models and AI-generated estimates. They are not derived from real-time data, verified research, or validated economic models unless explicitly stated.

4. Not Professional Advice

WhatIf is an exploratory tool. Its outputs must not be treated as:

  • Medical advice — Do not use WhatIf to make decisions about health, medication, diagnosis, or treatment.
  • Legal advice — WhatIf outputs do not constitute legal opinion. Consult a qualified legal professional for legal matters.
  • Financial or investment advice — WhatIf is not a financial instrument or authorised advisor. Do not make investment decisions based on WhatIf outputs.
  • Safety-critical decisions — WhatIf must not be used as the basis for decisions where errors could cause physical harm, loss of life, or safety failures.
  • Policy or governance decisions — WhatIf outputs may be used for exploratory thinking, but must not substitute for qualified expert analysis in real policy contexts.

5. Verify Before Acting

If you use WhatIf to explore a real decision:

  • Treat the output as a starting point for thinking, not a conclusion
  • Verify any specific facts, figures, or claims with authoritative and qualified sources
  • Consult relevant domain experts before acting on any significant decision
  • Do not share WhatIf outputs as verified facts without independent corroboration
